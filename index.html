01 / SERVICES

Technical support where the carbon curve is most vulnerable.

Focused work for projects that need a model built, a key assumption strengthened, or an independent view before financing, offtake, or validation.

01

Full ex-ante carbon model

EARLY-STAGE PROJECTS

Build a project-specific model from the ground up, covering baseline, growth, survival, mortality, uncertainty deductions, leakage, eligible pools, and year-by-year issuance trajectories.

02

Growth & survival modeling

FOCUSED TECHNICAL SUPPORT

Develop species-, site-, or ecosystem-specific growth trajectories, establishment survival, long-term mortality, high/base/low scenarios, and parameter uncertainty.

03

Independent technical diligence

INVESTORS / BUYERS / DEVELOPERS

Pressure-test assumptions, evidence, model structure, and projected delivery. Identify the parameters with the greatest influence on issuance and the weakest empirical support.

04

Climate stress testing

FORWARD-LOOKING RISK

Evaluate how plausible future climate conditions could alter growth, survival, mortality, and the timing or magnitude of projected credit delivery.

05

Allometry, biomass & custom analysis

SPECIALIST SUPPORT

Review or develop biomass relationships, propagate uncertainty from field measurements to carbon estimates, or tackle project-specific ecological and statistical modeling questions.

02 / WHO I WORK WITH

Project developers

Investors & buyers

03 / ABOUT

Forest science with an investment-side view.

RAT
R. Alex Thompson, PhD
Forest ecologist + quantitative scientist
North Carolina / working globally

I’m a forest ecologist and applied statistician. Most recently, I worked on the investment side at Rubicon Carbon, evaluating nature-based projects and the models used to forecast credit delivery.

My scientific background is in plant physiological ecology: how trees acquire, allocate, store, and lose carbon under changing environmental conditions. I bring that biological perspective into carbon-project modeling, where small assumptions about growth, survival, mortality, and biomass can materially change projected issuances.
