Case Study · Delivery Risk Analysis

Stress-testing a tropical reforestation carbon forecast.

Independent ecological diligence for a hypothetical ARR project in humid tropical Colombia, designed to determine which assumptions are supported by evidence, which materially affect delivery, and what happens when favorable assumptions compound across the model.

ColombiaTropical ARR40-year horizonBayesian modelingIndependent diligence
Illustrative case study. Project characteristics and developer assumptions are hypothetical. Empirical benchmarks are derived from published ecological datasets and literature. Secondary-forest observations are used as underwriting comparators and are not represented as direct measurements of planted ARR performance.
01 · Benchmark

Start with the assumptions.

A carbon model can be built from individually plausible inputs and still produce an aggressive forecast.

We benchmarked the developer's establishment survival, early biomass accumulation, mature biomass, stem mortality, and allometric model choice against published observations from Colombian and comparable Neotropical forests.

The 90% establishment-survival assumption exceeded the available restoration comparators. Early biomass at year 10 sat around the 76th percentile of the empirical benchmark, while year-40 biomass sat around the 73rd percentile. The assumed 1% annual mortality rate was lower than nearly all mature-forest comparators. By contrast, replacing the pantropical Chave allometry with a Colombia-specific regional relationship produced relatively small differences across the relevant tree-size range.

Not every optimistic assumption deserves equal attention.

The benchmark identified assumptions that were aggressive, assumptions that were relatively ordinary, and a plausible concern—choice of allometric equation—that appeared unlikely to materially alter the investment conclusion.

02 · Prioritize

Find the assumptions that actually move delivery.

Empirical optimism and delivery materiality are not the same thing.

We propagated the empirical input ranges through the ecological model and measured their approximate contribution to forecast variation at different underwriting horizons.

The dominant uncertainty changed with time. Early growth accounted for approximately 94% of modeled variation at year 10 and 42% at year 20, but became largely irrelevant later in the project. Mature biomass contributed approximately 37% at year 20, 73% at year 30, and 78% at year 40. Survival remained material throughout, while mortality increased in importance over time. Allometry contributed roughly 1% or less.

Diligence should follow materiality.

The most empirically aggressive assumption was not automatically the largest source of forecast uncertainty. Near-term diligence should focus heavily on early growth and establishment; long-dated delivery becomes increasingly dependent on the mature-biomass trajectory.

03 · Rebuild

Rebuild the trajectory from ecological evidence.

Individual assumption checks are useful. The investment question depends on what those assumptions do together.

We fit an evidence-constrained Bayesian Chapman–Richards growth model to humid and wet Neotropical secondary-forest observations. Biologically plausible priors define the parameter space, the empirical observations update those priors, and posterior uncertainty is retained rather than collapsed into a single deterministic growth rate.

The resulting comparison exposes a pattern that is difficult to see in a spreadsheet review. The developer trajectory sits substantially above the evidence-constrained central estimate across the project period, even though several of the underlying assumptions appear individually plausible.

The risk emerges from compounding favorable assumptions.

No single assumption needs to be obviously indefensible for the combined carbon trajectory to become aggressive. Rebuilding the forecast probabilistically makes that compounding visible and identifies the assumptions where project-specific evidence would most improve confidence.

The result

Plausible assumptions can still produce an aggressive forecast.

The analysis did not show that the project's ecological assumptions were universally unreasonable. It showed that several individually defensible assumptions compounded into a trajectory substantially above the central expectation supported by the broader empirical evidence. The diligence output is therefore more useful than a simple pass/fail judgment: it explains where the model is aggressive, what actually drives delivery, and what does not require additional attention.

A better basis for diligence

The useful questions are not only whether an assumption is possible, but whether it is supported by relevant evidence, how uncertain it is, how much that uncertainty matters to delivery, and what happens when favorable assumptions compound across the model.
